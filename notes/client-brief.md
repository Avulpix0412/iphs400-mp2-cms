# Client brief — The Bazaar Chinese community

The client is the Chinese-speaking player community of **The Bazaar** (大巴扎). It is not a formal organization, but it is a clear group. Right now its home base is **Bilibili**: a few top streamers (**up主**) and their fans. The streamers do live streams and post videos. Fans watch the streams or videos and talk in the comment sections.

## Who runs the site

The site would be run by those few top streamers. They are fairly well known and they already have experience managing fan groups. But they are not very comfortable with computers. They would be the **admins**.

Besides them, there would be some invited guide writers: other up主 or experienced players. They would be **editors**. They can write guides, but they can't manage users.

Writing in Markdown shouldn't be a big problem. Among all the up主 there will definitely be some who can do it, and even if none of them can, they can easily use AI to convert their text.

## Who reads it, and what they want

Anyone who plays the game. They just read; they don't need an account. The Bazaar has a strong guide culture. Compared with MOBAs or FPS games, a card game is more about strategy than mechanics, so players need guides more than in other games.

## What is broken right now

Everything is on Bilibili, and it is all video or live streams. A video is not like a written guide: you can't read it in a few spare minutes, or when you can't turn the sound on. Clear-cut information like **end-game builds** (终局阵容) is hard to find inside a video. Right now most streamers make guides by talking over a PowerPoint. A website with written guides (something like **旅法师营地**) would be a better channel for this. Comments and discussion can stay on Bilibili.

Timing is another big problem. The game gets **balance patches** that make cards stronger or weaker, on average about twice a month. Videos cost a lot to make (a single author might only post one or two a month), so they go out of date fast. When I look for a guide, unless it was just posted, it's hard to say how much it still applies.

## What would make them say "this is better"

They don't have a website yet. The biggest advantage of a website is that the information is easy to get to.

Say I want a guide for a specific hero on a specific patch. Right now I can only search Bilibili videos, and the videos are long, so it's hard to jump straight to the build I want. The Bazaar is a roguelike, so every run is different, and even inside one run the build keeps changing. That makes it really hard to get the information I need.

The existing guide videos are just someone talking over a PowerPoint. The useful information is only the pictures and what the up主 says. A post is clearly more direct: what they want to say can be written as text, and the pictures from the PowerPoint can go straight into the post. That is simpler for both the viewers and the people making guides.

The site would have three main pages in the menu: **Guides** (攻略), **Tournaments** (锦标赛), and **About** (关于). Guides can be browsed by **hero** or by **author**. Hero is the obvious one. I expect there won't be too many authors, so browsing by author works too.

## What must not happen

Editors and admins must have very different permissions. Admins should be able to control all users and all posts and pages. Editors should only be able to write guides. An editor must never be able to manage users or publish something that an admin hasn't reviewed: an editor's guide stays a draft until an admin checks it and publishes it.

This is not the final design yet, so this is what I have for now.
